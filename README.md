## Customer Churn Analysis Using Classification, Clustering, and Pattern Mining Techniques

### Project Overview
The project involves customer churn analysis using classification, clustering and association rule mining. Telco Customer Churn, a dataset consisting of 7,043 customers and 21 variables, was analysed to find out the pattern of the customers churning, predict those who will drop, segment customer groups, and uncover meaningful relationships between customer attributes. The machine learning models such as Decision Tree and K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) were created to predict churn. Customer segmentation was performed using the K-Means clustering method and frequent patterns of customer behavior were identified using the Apriori algorithm.

### Classification Analysis Insights
The two classification models, Decision Tree and KNN were implemented and tested with accuracy, F1 Score, Confusion Matrix, and ROC-AUC. The Decision Tree model performed best with accuracy of 79.42%, F1-score of 0.5821 and ROC-AUC scores of 0.8265. The Decision Tree model was able to serve better in the distinction between churn and non-churn customers than KNN. The classification results indicated a better prediction performance for retained customers as opposed to the churn prediction, which needed improvement due to class imbalance. The hyperparameter tuning was done using GridSearchCV with five-fold cross validation. The tuned Decision Tree model performance for churn detection was balanced while the original Decision Tree model performance showed better overall evaluation result.

### Clustering Analysis Insights
K-Means Clustering algorithm was used for customer segmentation. The four best customer segments found by silhouette score evaluation were those with a score of 0.2744. The four clusters were different customer characteristics by tenure, monthly charges, total charges, service usage and demographic attributes. Customer segmentation through cluster analysis was used to identify segments based on the likelihood of churn. Higher churn clusters were the ones that had a churn rate higher than 35%, which included Cluster 2 with 46.58% churn and Cluster 3 with 39.60% churn. These customer segments are high-risk segments that demand specific retention strategies and tailored engagement strategies.

### Patterns Mining Analysis Insights
The Apriori algorithm was used to perform association rule mining to identify relationships between customer services, contracts, billing methods, and churn behavior. The analysis resulted in 2100 frequent itemsets and 17511 association rules. Consistent churn signals were observed among customers that had month-to-month service agreements, fibre optic internet plans, no technical support, no security services, no protection for devices and no paperless billing plans. The relationships that are found can be useful to gain insights into the customer behavior that is linked to a higher churn risk.

### Practical Relevance and Real-World Applications
Patterns identified support strategies for customer retention as the ability to detect the high-risk customers for churn. Customer characteristics will be used to create custom offers, contract upgrade suggestions, customer support programs, and targeted communication campaigns for businesses. Customer segmentation enables a more personalized marketing approach because it helps to understand the behavior of each group, as well as their needs for retention. Proactive support can be provided to high risk customer segments and value added services and loyalty programmes can be implemented to loyal customer segments. Association rule mining can then be used to gain insights that can help with service recommendations, business decision making, and predicting customer retention or churn. A combination of classification, clustering and pattern mining offers a data-driven solution for enhancing Customer Satisfaction, minimizing Customer Losing and maximizing the Business Value for the long run.

### Challenges Encountered and Solutions
Class imbalance between churn and non-churn customers was a major challenge during classification modeling. The F1 score and ROC-AUC metrics were used for performance evaluation, which gave improved assessment results than the accuracy alone. Customer attributes were categorized and after that transformed to enable the implementation of machine learning. The categorical variables were converted into numerical values using OneHotEncoder for model training. The relationship between customers and customer behaviour was complex making it difficult to discern meaningful patterns. Clustering and association rule mining methods were used to identify groups of customers and associations among their behaviours. The other challenge encountered was hyperparameter selection, which was solved using GridSearchCV optimization. A suitable parameter for the Decision Tree model was determined and the performance improvement of models was assessed using cross validation.

### Conclusion
The project was able to apply classification, clustering and pattern mining methods to the analysis of customer churn behavior successfully. Decision Tree was found to be the best predictive model for classification. K-Means clustering was used to segment the customers into meaningful groups, and the Apriori association rule mining identified meaningful customer behavior patterns. The analytical results offer real-world business benefits, such as customer retention planning, targeted marketing approaches and data-informed decision making. The developed approach proves the effectiveness of machine learning and data mining techniques in customer relationships management.
